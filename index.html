CW Lead took the worst of it: 658 of its 830 August leads were 65-plus, 79%. That explains the flat $1.25 cost per lead and the slide from 4.1% to about 1% qualified. Its non-retiree age buckets produced zero qualified leads in August. Retirees were not part of the problem there, they were all of it.

The mechanism, and the fix

Every ad set with a 65 ceiling also had Advantage Audience switched on. That setting lets Meta deliver outside your stated age range, ceiling or no ceiling. That is how 65-plus got in. Those ad sets are paused across CW Lead, IW Lead and DHW Lead, every live ad set is 25-55 with Advantage Audience off, and delivery confirms it: zero 65-plus since Aug 20.

One correction to the fix: 55 is too tight

55 was the emergency setting and it cuts too deep. 55-64 is the strongest cohort in the account everywhere it runs. The bad line is between 55-64 and 65-plus, not at 55.

Flagging this because it looks worse than it is. The three custom conversions that count a qualified subscriber are written loosely, so on paper any completed registration would count. In practice the numbers are right, because the filtering happens upstream, before Meta ever sees the event.

If the loose rule were counting every signup, that second number would be near 632. It is 57. So the cost-per-qualified-lead figures on this page hold.

The risk is fragility. Nothing inside Meta enforces that the upstream feed only ever fires this event for qualified people. If that changes, the number quietly turns to noise and every optimisation follows it down. We own that feed, so I will tighten the rule on our side. Housekeeping, not a repair.
